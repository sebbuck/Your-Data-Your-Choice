<header class="page-header"><p class="eyebrow">TRANSPARENCY</p><h1>How we build trust.</h1><p>Digital rights education should be useful, evidence-led and honest about uncertainty.</p></header>
<div class="prose"><h2>Our standards</h2><ul><li>Use plain language and explain technical terms.</li><li>Prioritize legislation, regulators, official documentation and credible research.</li><li>Include publication or review dates on substantive articles as the library grows.</li><li>Distinguish facts, interpretation, examples and opinion.</li><li>Avoid fear-based claims, unsupported rankings and claims that imply every service behaves the same way.</li><li>Disclose funding, partnerships and conflicts of interest.</li></ul><h2>Corrections</h2><p>Readers can suggest corrections through the repository's public issue tracker. Substantive corrections should be reflected transparently in the relevant page.</p><h2>Limitations</h2><p>Privacy law and technology change. Content is general education, not legal advice. Readers should check current primary sources for decisions that affect them.</p></div>
