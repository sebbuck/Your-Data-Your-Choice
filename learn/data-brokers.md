<header class="page-header"><p class="eyebrow">YOUR DATA IN ACTION · 7 MIN READ</p><h1>What are data brokers?</h1><p>Data brokers are organizations that collect, combine, analyse or share information about people, often outside a direct relationship with those people.</p></header>
<article class="prose"><h2>Where can information come from?</h2><p>Depending on the organization and applicable law, sources may include public records, commercial partners, surveys, online activity, apps, purchases or other datasets. The details vary widely.</p><h2>Why combine datasets?</h2><p>Combining records can help create audience segments, identity links, risk indicators or predictions. These profiles may be used for marketing, analytics, identity verification or other purposes. Not every broker performs every activity.</p><h2>Questions worth asking</h2><ul><li>What information does the organization hold?</li><li>Where did it come from, and what is it used for?</li><li>Who receives it, and how long is it retained?</li><li>What access, correction, deletion or opt-out rights apply in your situation?</li></ul><h2>Practical steps</h2><p>Review privacy settings on services you use, limit optional sharing where appropriate, and look for privacy request processes. In Canada, rights and obligations depend on the organization, province and activity. Start with the <a href="{{ '/rights/canada/' | relative_url }}">Canadian privacy rights guide</a>.</p><p class="source-note">This guide is an introduction, not a directory or rating of data brokers. Verify organization-specific claims using current primary sources.</p></article>
