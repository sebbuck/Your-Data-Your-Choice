<header class="page-header"><p class="eyebrow">YOUR DATA IN ACTION · 6 MIN READ</p><h1>How does targeted advertising work?</h1><p>Many digital ads are selected using signals about context, audiences and predicted relevance.</p></header>
<article class="prose"><h2>Signals and segments</h2><p>Advertising systems may use information such as page context, broad interests, approximate location, device information or activity on a service. Some systems group people into audience segments based on observed or inferred characteristics.</p><h2>How an ad is selected</h2><p>When an ad space becomes available, automated systems may evaluate eligible campaigns and select an ad using factors such as relevance, bid, campaign settings and platform rules. The process can happen quickly and differs between services.</p><h2>What can you control?</h2><ul><li>Review ad-personalization controls in the services you use.</li><li>Check browser and mobile privacy settings.</li><li>Limit optional tracking permissions where available.</li><li>Understand that turning off personalization may not remove ads or all data collection.</li></ul><div class="callout"><strong>A useful distinction:</strong> Seeing an ad does not prove exactly what data was used to select it. Check the platform's own explanation and current settings.</div></article>
